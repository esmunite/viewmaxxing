How grading works

Every video gets two scores, each out of 100. They're split on purpose: a beautiful video aimed at the wrong audience should score high on craft and low on strategy — and you should be able to see that.

Creative quality — how good the video itself is

The craft, judged against what the best short-form creators do. Built from six dimensions:

  • Hook — do the first 1–3 seconds make a specific promise to a specific viewer? A greeting ("hey guys") is not a hook.
  • Visuals — framing, lighting, visual variety, 9:16 filled with no black bars.
  • Pacing — a cut or change every 1–3 seconds, no dead air.
  • Audio — voice clarity, levels, music and sound choices.
  • Platform fit — does it look and feel native to the platforms you picked?
  • On-screen text — captions readable on a phone screen, placed well.

Goal alignment — how well it serves your plan

This is where your Creative Brief comes in. Bennett checks the video against the goal you picked (virality, conversion, growth, or engagement), the platforms you selected, and the viewer you described. Same video, different goal — different score.

The rubric

ScoreMeaning
85+Exceptional — would stand out in a competitive feed
60–84Solid, with clear gaps to fix
40–59Average and forgettable
Below 40Fundamental problems

Most videos land between 30 and 60. The scores are deliberately tough — an 85 should feel earned.

Hook frameworks Bennett looks for

Your opening is graded against seven proven archetypes: pattern interrupt, open loop, bold or negative claim, numbered promise, direct question, in-medias-res, and relatable POV. Your fixes name the exact framework to use, with a concrete rewrite — not generic advice.

Honest limits

  • No virality predictions. We don't have historical performance data, so Bennett won't guess your view count. Anyone who claims to predict that is guessing.
  • 3 free grades per day, resetting at midnight UTC.
  • Visual analysis watches your actual video frames. If the vision model is ever down, you'll see a notice and get a transcript-only grade instead — it'll say so, not pretend.
